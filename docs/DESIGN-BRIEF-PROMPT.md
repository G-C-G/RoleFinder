# Prompt for a design review of GCG Game Plan

Attach `GCG-Game-Plan-screens.pdf` (or the screenshots) and paste this:

---

I run Gold Coast Games (GCG), a small events company in Accra, Ghana, that runs game nights and corporate game experiences. Attached are phone screenshots of our team app, **GCG Game Plan**, with what each screen does and why.

**The whole point of the app:** a busy, mostly unpaid team member opens it and within 30 seconds knows three things, without feeling overwhelmed:

1. What is GCG doing right now?
2. Where do I fit?
3. What is the one thing I need to do next?

**The rule that matters most:** people must never see the whole event plan at once. They should see only the portion that matters now: the tasks for the current stage of the event, ordered by priority and deadline. Everything later stays hidden until they choose to look. Finishing what is in front of them should feel like clearing a level and moving to the next one, not like a long to-do list. Events move through five stages: Plan, Prep, Check, Play, Report.

**Onboarding is creating your profile:** a short card game (situations, jobs that might suit you) that produces a player card, then the profile: what you have done, what you bring, your proudest work, your CV (link or pasted text), a photo if you like, how you work best and why you are here. It must feel welcoming and personal, save progress so people can stop and come back, and never feel like an HR form.

**What already exists:** a Home with one "next move", a "then" line and a note about later tasks; events shown as a five-stage journey; task cards with a deadline, why you were picked, one big action, and quieter "can't do it" and "not really me" options; a private profile with a shareable player card and a record of everything you have done at GCG; and an admin side for me (Today, Events, Team, Insights). The visual style: warm cream background, red, gold and green bands from the Ghana flag, chunky outlined game-piece shapes, playful but professional.

**Please:**

1. Tell me where the current screens still feel overwhelming or unclear, screen by screen, and why.
2. Show how to make "only what matters now" even stronger: what a member sees at each stage of an event, and how finishing a stage should feel.
3. Suggest how onboarding and profile creation can feel quicker and more personal, including the photo and CV steps, without losing the information we need.
4. Keep it simple: no new features unless they remove effort. No points, badges, leaderboards, chat or availability tracking.
5. Finish with a precise design brief for my developer (an AI coding assistant): screen by screen, what to show, what to hide, the order of elements, and the one primary action on each screen. Phone first, 390px wide.
